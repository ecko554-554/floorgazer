## 🧭 What floorgazer Actually Does

If you are new to this world, here is the short version.

An **NFT collection** is a set of digital collectibles that share a theme — a series of images, trading cards, or digital items. Each one can be bought and sold. The **floor price** is the cheapest one currently for sale in that collection. It is the single number most people watch, because it tells you, at a glance, what it would cost to join that collection today.

floorgazer watches those floor prices for you. Instead of opening a website, searching a collection, and squinting at a chart every hour, you keep a small window open and the numbers update on their own.

## 🚀 Getting Started After the First Launch

The first time you open floorgazer, the main window is empty — that is expected. Nothing is broken.

**1. Add your first collection.**
Look for the add button or the search field. Type the name of the collection you want to follow, select it from the results, and confirm. The collection now appears in your list with its current floor price.

**2. Add a few more.**
Repeat for any other collections you care about. Two to six is a comfortable number for most people — enough to be useful, not so many that the window becomes cluttered.

**3. Arrange your view.**
Resize the window and drag it wherever it is convenient. Many users park it in a corner of the screen while they work.

**4. Set how often it refreshes.**
In the settings area you can choose how frequently floorgazer checks for new prices. A shorter interval means fresher numbers; a longer interval means less network traffic. Something in the middle is usually the right balance.

**5. Leave it running.**
Close the settings, minimise the window, and get on with your day. The numbers will be waiting for you when you look back.

## 📊 How to Read What You See

**Floor price** — the cheapest listing right now. This is the headline number.

**Change over time** — the difference between the floor price now and the floor price earlier. A green or upward arrow means the cheapest listing has become more expensive; a red or downward arrow means it has become cheaper.

**Currency** — prices are typically shown in the network's native currency, most often ETH (Ether). Do not confuse a price in ETH with a price in dollars — they are very different numbers.

**Last updated** — a timestamp showing when the app last checked. If it looks stale, your internet connection or the refresh interval is usually the reason.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting

**The window opens but the list stays empty.**
Check your internet connection first. Then use the search field to add a collection manually — the app cannot show you anything until you tell it what to follow.

**Prices have not changed in a while.**
Open settings and check the refresh interval. A very long interval can make it look like nothing is happening. You can also force a manual refresh from the menu.

**Windows blocks the program.**
Click **More info** on the warning box, then **Run anyway**. This is standard for small open-source tools that are not sold through the Microsoft Store.

**The app uses more memory than expected.**
Try removing collections you no longer follow and lowering the refresh frequency. Fewer collections and longer intervals mean less work.

**You downloaded it twice and now there are two copies.**
Delete one. Running two copies at once can make both of them behave strangely.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a crypto wallet to use this?**
No. floorgazer reads publicly available price data and does not require a wallet, an account, or a login.

**Is it really free?**
Yes. It is open source with no paid tiers, no subscriptions, and no advertising.

**Will it work on a Mac?**
This version is built for Windows. Mac and Linux users would need a different build.

**Does it trade for me?**
No. It is a tracker and a viewer. It never places orders or moves funds on your behalf.

**Can I use it offline?**
No. It needs an internet connection to fetch current prices.

**How often should I refresh?**
Every few minutes is plenty for most people. Very fast refreshing gains you little and creates extra network traffic.
